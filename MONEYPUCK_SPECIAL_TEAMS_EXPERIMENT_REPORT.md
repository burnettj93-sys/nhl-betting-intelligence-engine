foundation (`MONEYPUCK_TEAM_INGESTION_REPORT.md`) never mapped `iceTime`

prefers a genuine TOI-based rate ("xGF per 60") over a fabricated
opportunity count, and a direct "PP opportunities" column is confirmed
(again, this turn) not reliably available from MoneyPuck. Rather than
either fabricate an opportunity count or fall back to a cruder per-game
average, `ice_time_seconds` was added as a minimal, targeted schema/
ingestion extension: `schema.sql` gained one column, `ingest.py` gained
one field mapping (`iceTime` → `ice_time_seconds`, in raw SECONDS, no
unit conversion at ingestion time), and the already-archived raw file
(same SHA-256, no new download, no new network contact) was re-ingested
into a freshly-created `research_moneypuck.db` — verified: 0 rows with
NULL `ice_time_seconds` among the 20,992 special-teams rows, and the
existing cross-validation (100.0000% NHL-corpus coverage, 0 team
mismatches, the same 373 documented shootout score discrepancies) was
re-run and reconfirmed unchanged. All 33 pre-existing ingestion tests
pass unmodified.

## B. Exact PP formula

```
PP xGF/60 = ( Σ xg_for  over the most recent `window` games at 5on4 ) * 3600
            ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Σ ice_time_seconds over those same games
```

## C. Exact PK formula

```
PK xGA/60 = ( Σ xg_against over the most recent `window` games at 4on5 ) * 3600
            ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Σ ice_time_seconds over those same games
```
Lower PK xGA/60 = a **better** (tighter) penalty kill.

## D. Rolling windows tested

`{10, 25}` — same two-value grid as the prior two experiments, per Part
4's explicit example scale.

## E. Maturity / sample policy

Two conditions, both required (Part 5):
1. **Full window of games required** — same no-partial-window policy as
   the team-level xG features.
2. **Minimum accumulated TOI floor**: `MIN_TOTAL_TOI_SECONDS = 1200`
   (20 minutes). Real corpus check this turn: mean PP TOI per game ≈
   4.58 minutes (10,496 real 5on4 rows, 2022-23→2025-26), so `window=10`
   accumulates ~46 minutes on average — the floor is a genuine safety
   backstop for a sparse-penalty stretch (Part 5's explicit "do not
   treat 3 power plays as mature"), not the primary gate, and rarely
   binds in practice.

## F. Season-boundary policy

Identical no-cross-season-carryover policy as the team-level xG
features, for the same simplicity/auditability reasons (Part 7)
documented in `research/moneypuck_team_features.py` and reused here in
`research/moneypuck_special_teams_features.py`.

## G. Matchup formula

Part 12: opponent-interactive terms, not simple team-vs-team subtraction.

```
term_home = home_PP_xGF60 − away_PK_xGA60
term_away = away_PP_xGF60 − home_PK_xGA60
```
`term_home` is large and positive specifically when the home team's own
PP is strong **and** this particular away opponent's PK is weak — a
genuine matchup quantity, not two independent team ratings. Returns
`None` if any of the 4 underlying rates is immature — a matchup term is
only as mature as its least-mature ingredient.

**Candidate definitions (Part 3):**
- **B** (PP only, team-level): `home_PP_xGF60 − away_PP_xGF60`
- **C** (PK only, team-level): `away_PK_xGA60 − home_PK_xGA60` (positive favors home)
- **D** (PP+PK matchup differential, single scalar): `term_home − term_away`
- **E** (compact composite): `(term_home, term_away)` fit as **two separate** standardized coefficients, letting the logistic fit learn different weights for each matchup direction instead of assuming symmetry

## X. PP/PK stability findings (Part 22)

This is the most informative part of the experiment, and explains *why*
the candidates above failed rather than just *that* they failed:

- **Rolling-window volatility**: mean coefficient of variation of
  window-10 PP xGF/60 across the tuning season, per team (32 teams with
  sufficient samples): **0.268** — a team's own rolling PP rate swings by
  roughly ±27% of its mean over the course of a season. Noisy, though
  not wildly so on its own.
- **Season-to-season persistence**: Pearson correlation between a team's
  final window-25 PP xGF/60 in 2023-24 and its final window-25 PP xGF/60
  in the following season, 2024-25 (31 teams with both values,
  independently season-scoped, no carryover): **r = 0.105**. This is
  **very weak, close to no persistence at all**. A team that had a
  strong power play last season is barely more likely than chance to
  have one again this season, by this measure.

**This is the real finding of this experiment, reported plainly per
Part 22's instruction rather than forced into the model anyway**: rolling
team-level PP xGF/60 (and, by the same reasoning and its high
correlation with PP in Section Y, PK xGA/60) does not behave like a
stable team skill at the level this project can currently measure it —
it behaves substantially like noise from one season to the next. That
is a coherent, evidence-backed explanation for why B and C carried a
small *negative* out-of-sample effect (a noisy signal, once weighted by
a coefficient fit on a different, equally noisy sample, actively hurts
more than it helps) and why D's matchup construction was assigned
essentially zero weight by the fitting process itself.

## AC. Recommendation

```
KEEP CURRENT MODEL.
```

This is the clearest negative result of the three experiments run so
far, and it is checked against all 9 of Part 30's conditions:
1. Brier improvement — **fails** for B/C/E (all worse); D is not
   distinguishable from zero effect (Δ = -0.000006).
2. Log-loss neutral/improved — same as above.
3. Persists across both seasons — **fails** for B/C/E (consistently
   worse in both, not just one); D is consistently ~zero.
4. Reasonable calibration — not applicable to D (no real adjustment is
   being made); moot for B/C/E, which fail earlier conditions.
5. Controlled extremity — no concern for D; moot for the others.
6. Meaningful (not microscopic) effect — **fails outright**. D's effect
   is not merely small, it is statistically and numerically
   indistinguishable from a no-op.
7. Credible bootstrap support — **fails**: D's interval is tight around
   zero; B/C/E mostly sit on the "worse" side.
8. Not merely duplicating Elo/5v5 xG — **fails for B and C** (r up to
   0.56 with signals already tested); D and E are novel but carry no
   signal anyway.
9. Clean temporal integrity — **pass** for all candidates.

Per Part 31: this negative result is not a case for "keep tuning
increasingly complicated PP/PK formulas." The stability finding
(Section X — season-to-season persistence r=0.105) gives a real,
evidence-based reason to expect that outcome to hold for other PP/PK
formulations built the same way (rolling team-level rate over a game
window), not just this specific candidate set.

## AD. Recommended next single development slice

Two candidate directions were explicitly authorized for future
consideration (Part 31), plus the exploratory secondary comparison Part
10 permitted but did not require:

**Primary recommendation: team shot-quality offense and defense
decomposed separately** (rather than the already-tested combined
xGF-xGA differential) — this is a genuinely different formulation of the
5v5/all-situation signal, not another special-teams variant, and
directly follows from this experiment's own finding that *team-level*
rolling rates over a game window can carry real but weak signal (5v5 xG
share did) or effectively none (special teams did) depending on how
stable the underlying quantity actually is — offense and defense
decomposed separately is worth testing specifically *because* it might
reveal that one side of the puck is more stable/predictive than the
other, where the combined differential mixed a stable and a noisy
component together.

**Exploratory only, not the headline decision (Part 10)**: a quick check
of "current model + best 5v5 xG research candidate + special teams" vs.
"current model + best 5v5 xG research candidate" alone was **not run
this turn** — Part 10 permits it only *after* the primary experiment
concluded, and given special teams showed no exploitable signal on its
own (Section X's persistence finding suggests it wouldn't meaningfully
change in combination either), running it would very likely just
reproduce this report's null result at added complexity. Flagged here
as available but not executed, consistent with not chasing a result the
evidence already argues against.

## AE. Should the first visual dashboard begin now?

**Yes — with an explicit research-process framing, not a "trust this
number" framing.** Three independent-data experiments have now been run
(Elo, team xG, special teams) with consistent methodological rigor
(real data, STRICT PRIOR-GAME-DATE, tuning/eval separation, paired
bootstrap, honest negative-result reporting) and a consistent
conclusion: the production model has not yet been beaten, but the
*process* for testing whether it can be is now mature, repeatable, and
well-instrumented. A dashboard that visualizes **that process** —
calibration curves, backtest results, feature coverage, the experiment
history itself — is a legitimate and valuable next artifact right now,
independent of whether any individual feature has been adopted yet.
What remains premature is a dashboard framed as "here is today's
trustworthy win probability" for end-user consumption, since no
real-data-tested enhancement has yet cleared adoption. The distinction
matters and should be made explicit in scoping that slice, not treated
as a blanket yes/no.
