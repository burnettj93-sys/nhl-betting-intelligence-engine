# Eggy intelligence

Eggy decides in this order, for every bet it might record or show, and never the reverse:

> understand the game, then the player, then the market, then why the model disagrees with the market, then argue against the bet, then ask whether the thesis survives, and only then rank and record.

The old learning-book engine ran the opposite way (eligible, edge, sort, bet), and on 2026-10-20 it recorded ten bets that a careful look at the data on disk would have passed on (`docs/validation/eggy_intelligence_oct20.md`). This document says what the replacement reads, what it decides and why, how it is judged afterwards, and what it does not know.

No new API, database, scheduler, ingestion or market was added. Everything below reads files the platform already held.

## 1. The review card

`operational/eggy_intelligence.py` produces one card per candidate bet. It is a plain dict stored with the bet (`intelligence_json`) and shown in the app. Its sections are the ones the owner asked for: the bet (player or team, market, threshold, price, with the market key the provider's archived payload actually carried), the most likely hockey outcome, why tonight, the player or team thesis, the model's probability, the market's probability and the disagreement, why they disagree, the case for, the case against, historical support with sample sizes, the model state, data completeness (present and missing), contradiction level, calibration confidence, final decision confidence and the action (RECORD, LOWER_PRIORITY or PASS).

`answers` restates the card as the thirteen things a reader should get at a glance: why this team or player, why tonight, why this market, why this price, who is most likely to win, what the model believes, what the market believes, why they disagree, what supports it, what argues against it, what history says, what data is missing and what would make Eggy pass. They are assembled from the card's own fields, so the two cannot disagree.

There is **no intelligence score**. A score needs weights, and no history can say what they should be. Every judgment is a rule on a named, printed quantity; where a number can be checked against history it is, with its sample size; where it cannot (is the market wrong about a moneyline?) the card says UNVALIDATED.

## 3. The decision rules

**Thesis types.** Moneyline: `MODEL_SEES_VALUE` (the hockey model is 3 or more points above the market's no-vig probability), `MODEL_SAYS_OVERPRICED` (3 or more below), and within 3 points of the market: `COIN_FLIP_AT_FAIR_PRICE` (the model's probability is within 3 points of 50%: no better team to argue for or against), `LIKELY_WINNER_AT_FAIR_PRICE` (the hockey favourite) or `UNDERDOG_AT_FAIR_PRICE` (the hockey underdog). Prop: `MODEL_SEES_VALUE`, `FAIR_PRICED`, `PRICE_ABOVE_ESTIMATE`. The estimate used for expected value on a moneyline is the market's own no-vig probability, because nothing shows a model beating it; Elo plays no part in any decision.

**Contradiction level** is a count, not a weighted sum: one MODERATE flag is MODERATE; two MODERATE or one HIGH is HIGH; two HIGH or any SEVERE is SEVERE. Flags name the quantity they rest on: `HOCKEY_PICTURE_AGAINST`, `MARKET_MAY_KNOW_MORE` (a large model-market gap is suspicion, not confidence), `MODEL_NOT_SHOWN_TO_BEAT_MARKET` (the model's paired log-loss advantage over the market on the games priced so far is not at least two standard errors: a gap of a few thousandths on 87 games is noise, so this does not flip with each day's results), `LONG_PRICE` (the owner's skepticism ladder: a +150 or longer moneyline and a +300 prop are MODERATE, a +300 moneyline and a +600 prop HIGH), `MODEL_ABOVE_RECORD`, `ROLE_ASSUMPTION`, `POWER_PLAY_ASSUMPTION`, `LOW_USAGE`, `GAME_ENVIRONMENT`, `HISTORY_SAYS_MODEL_RUNS_HIGH`, `LONGSHOT_TAIL`, `THIN_HISTORY`, `MODEL_FAR_ABOVE_MARKET`, `PRICE_ABOVE_ESTIMATE`.

**Calibration confidence** is HIGH when 1,000 or more historical analogues exist and the model's average sat within two standard errors of what happened (or the estimate was recalibrated to such a cell); MEDIUM at 300 or more; LOW otherwise; INSUFFICIENT with no analogue. A longshot (price implying 5% or less, +1900 and longer) needs HIGH calibration and a 100-game record or it is capped at LOW.

**Recalibration can only lower an estimate.** The estimate is the leg's own (for shots, the player's record held to the model plus at most 0.02: the anchor policy's cap, which stays). Where the single strongest cell with 300 or more cases and a gap of two standard errors says the model ran HIGH for players like this, the estimate is lowered to what happened (never below a quarter of the model's number, and the standard error is the one under what the model said, so a cell with no events cannot produce a near-zero error). Where history says the model ran LOW, the estimate is NOT raised: a recalibration that lifted an estimate above the leg's own number would manufacture an edge the capped estimate does not have, and the card says so (`HISTORY_SAYS_MODEL_RUNS_LOW`, level LOW). Out of time (cells trained on games before 2026-02-01, scored on 20,970 skater-games after it) the lowering rule is calibration-neutral: Brier moves by no more than 0.00003 in six of the seven markets and improves points 1+ by 0.00014 (`docs/validation/eggy_prop_calibration_replay.json`). An earlier symmetric version improved shots 3+ by 0.0008, but only by raising estimates above the cap, which is not allowed; it over-corrected some goal bands in both directions.

**Calibration confidence** treats a model that ran LOW in a cell as conservative, not as miscalibrated (a capped estimate cannot be overstated by it), and a model that ran HIGH two-sidedly; the card says so. For a lowered estimate it is HIGH only when a cell specific to the player (his ice time, his power play, his team's environment) has 1,000 or more cases; the population-level probability band alone gives MEDIUM, because it vouches for the model in the band and not for him.

**Actions.** A PASS is never recorded. A RECORD needs the thesis to survive: for a prop, 3 or more points over the price, contradiction no worse than MODERATE, calibration at least MEDIUM, and a longshot needs HIGH calibration and the long record. A fair-priced prop or favourite with a well-calibrated estimate is LOWER_PRIORITY (a control). A price above the estimate is LOWER_PRIORITY only as a bounded experiment (the event at least 25% likely, the price within 10 points of the estimate, calibration at least MEDIUM) and otherwise a PASS. A moneyline model edge is a RECORD only once the model has led the market on the games priced so far by at least two standard errors (paired per-game log loss); until then it is a LOWER_PRIORITY experiment, and the card says why. On the 87 games priced so far the market was slightly ahead (log loss 0.6438 market, 0.6467 hockey model, 0.6456 goalie-team strength, 0.6726 Elo; paired difference z = -0.2). A LOWER_PRIORITY bet is recorded only in the last 25 minutes before its own game and only inside the strict wave caps.

**Ranking** is lexicographic: action, edge class, contradiction, calibration confidence, hit chance to the nearest point, edge, then the bet's key. A game that already holds three of the day's bets sends its further RECORD candidates behind every other game's; a LOWER_PRIORITY candidate is simply not recorded past three, and a game holds at most one negative-edge experiment a day (fewer than ten bets is a correct answer).

**Edge classes** use the main engine's own 3-point threshold (they used 2, which is the root of the underdog bias below). POSITIVE_EDGE needs 3 points and calibration at least MEDIUM; NEGATIVE is 3 points or worse; a moneyline is judged on the hockey model's edge, not the market-anchored estimate.

**Fail closed.** If the evidence file is missing or more than 21 days old, or either state is missing or its newest game is more than 3 days before the date being reviewed, the cohort records nothing and says why. It never falls back to unreviewed ranking. The evidence file's hash is part of the policy digest and is named on every card, so a rebuilt file needs a fresh approval and bets made under different evidence can be told apart. An engine error in one candidate's review costs only that candidate and is reported as an error (never as "passed on judgment").

## 4. The underdog bias, and its mathematical reason

The old engine's edge on a moneyline was the market's no-vig probability less the price's implied probability. For a proportional margin that is `-p x (overround - 1)`: the side's own share of the margin, linear in its probability, with no hockey in it. On the 87 games the shadow log priced the mean overround was 1.0433, so the class flips at a no-vig probability of 46.2%. Of the 133 eligible sides (-150 or better), every one of the 48 favourites was classed NEGATIVE; 66 of the 85 underdogs were NEUTRAL, the preferred class. The ranking then promoted NEUTRAL over NEGATIVE, the -150 floor removed the heavy favourites, and Elo (the worst-calibrated estimate) was the "model" probability. Underdogs were favoured by arithmetic alone. The fix does not ban underdogs: an underdog the hockey model rates as underpriced is reviewed on the same terms and can be recorded or held lower, with the hockey picture declared against it. Detail: `docs/validation/eggy_moneyline_replay.json`.

## 5. Judging the decision, apart from the outcome

Every bet has two results: the OUTCOME (win, loss, push) and the DECISION (GOOD, QUESTIONABLE, BAD, INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE). `operational/eggy_decisions.py` judges the decision from the pre-bet card alone, then uses the outcome only to check the card's factual claims (the ice time, the power-play role, who controlled the game), to ask whether a loss is NORMAL_VARIANCE, and to say what Eggy should learn.

| | Won | Lost |
|---|---|---|
| **Good decision** | The expected reward of a sound process; confidence unchanged | NORMAL_VARIANCE, only if earned (below); otherwise a named cause |
| **Questionable** | No reward; confidence not increased | No punishment for the loss; the doubts were real |
| **Bad** | LUCK: confidence must not rise, the process still needs fixing | A process failure; the loss itself is not blamed, the decision is |

NORMAL_VARIANCE is earned only when the thesis was defensible, calibration was at least MEDIUM, no data integrity defect exists, no factual claim failed, and the outcome was not rarer than 1 in 10 by Eggy's own estimate; the postmortem then states each of those. A specific cause the box score shows (the ice time that did not happen, the power play that did not exist) is never overruled. A bet recorded before the review existed is reconstructed from its own day's data and labelled RECONSTRUCTED.

## 6. Where it shows

Opening any learning-book bet on the Paper Performance page, or any leg of Eggy's pick or best candidate on the Slate Board, shows the card and, after the game, the two results, what happened, whether each claim in the thesis held, what to learn, the classification and the action. Every bet is named completely everywhere: `Filip Chytil — SHOTS ON GOAL 2+ — -130`, `Jamie Oleksiak — GOALS 1+ — +2500`, `VAN — MONEYLINE — +240` (`operational/bet_labels.py`).

## 7. What did not change

The 10-bet learning book: 10 a day as a target, $10 each, -150 or better, 14 Eastern days, the $500 paper bankroll with logged resets that are funding and never profit, price age as metadata, automatic settlement, and the owner's recording pause. Eggy's own selection and policy digest (one official pick a day) are untouched: its legs gain a review card for display, and a leg the review would pass is shown as a pass rather than hidden.

## 8. Limits

- The team model has not been shown to beat the market (market log loss 0.6438 against 0.6467 on 87 games; the 95% range for the weight a disagreement deserves is -1.2 to 1.96). It is used to understand the matchup and to scrutinise disagreement, never as proof the market is wrong. Replay by disagreement size found no signal either way (sides the model liked by 3 or more points won 31 against 32.6 expected).
- Starters, lineups and injuries are missing, and the card says so every time. The market knows them.
- Eggy's reviews of past tickets are not kept (the ticket ledger has no review column); the learning book's reviews are.
- Eighty-seven games and a few hundred bets are small samples; same-game bets are one cluster.
- The evidence file is a snapshot (data through 2026-10-20). Re-run `deploy/build_eggy_evidence.py` after the MoneyPuck files change; the layer refuses to run on an evidence file more than 21 days old.
- The half-life (30 games) and shrink (10 games) of the team model are fixed constants chosen in exploratory work on 2014-2016; the builder re-derives only the feature set.
- A replayed decision uses the evidence file as it stands today, which includes the replayed day's own results (the skater cells by under 1% of their rows, the model-against-market record by one day of 87 games); the significance rule keeps that from flipping a decision on noise.
- `model_probability` on a moneyline row is Elo for the four Oct 20 bets and the first Oct 21 bet (recorded before this release) and the hockey-model probability afterwards.

## 10. Learning parlays (the learning unit, 2026-10-21)

The reviewed candidate legs are the raw material; the learning unit is a PARLAY (`operational/learning_parlays.py`, recorded in the cohort ledger's `parlays` table, up to 10 a day, none forced). No new scoring framework, data, API, scheduler or database.

- **A leg qualifies** only if the hockey/player review survives it (contradiction LOW or MODERATE once the straight bet's price-versus-estimate flags are set aside, calibration at least MEDIUM) and the event is likely (estimate 55% or more). No lottery legs; no leg is added to lengthen the payout.
- **3 legs are preferred.** A 4th is added only if it independently passes the same review, is at least as strong as the ticket's weakest leg, and adds no strong dependence.
- **Weakest leg** = the lowest estimate. It sets the quality: GOOD needs it at 62% or better and at most one MODERATE doubt; BAD (any leg under 55% or a HIGH doubt) is a PASS.
- **Dependence** comes from the existing `eggy_candidates.classify_pair`. Different games: ASSUMED INDEPENDENT ABSENT KNOWN SHARED DEPENDENCE (an assumption, not a finding), probability = product of the leg estimates. Two legs of one game are never multiplied: the combined probability is NOT GIVEN, only assumption-free Frechet bounds are, and an unclear pair (opposing players) is labelled UNCERTAIN. One player twice, a goal and a point, or both sides of a game are refused.
- **Price.** No combined sportsbook price exists anywhere in the system, so every parlay is an UNPRICED LEARNING PARLAY. The product of the legs' own quoted prices is kept as a labelled reference, never a quote; nothing is staked and no return or ROI is claimed. The ticket is settled by its legs (HIT / MISS / VOID / UNRESOLVED) and records the leg(s) that killed it. The $500 bankroll is untouched by parlays.
- **Eggy's flagship** is the strongest GOOD ticket of the day by the same order (quality, fewer legs, strongest weakest leg, likelier). It is shown as a proposal only; Eggy's official pick and its policy digest are unchanged until the owner approves wiring it in.
- **Postmortem**: a PARLAY SUMMARY (legs, priced or not, hit or miss, weakest leg, killers, GOOD / QUESTIONABLE / BAD PARLAY judged from the construction, never the result) sits above the unchanged leg-level reviews, which every leg keeps.
- **No price floor on a leg** (owner 2026-10-21). A leg qualifies on the review, calibrated probability (>= 0.55, calibration >= MEDIUM), role/data completeness, non-price contradiction <= MODERATE, then weakest-leg quality and dependence. Its price is stored and enters the ticket's reference product; the +100 objective belongs to a finished PRICED parlay. The only price check is validity (an American price inside (-100, +100) is impossible). No extreme-price bound is added: an invented cut would be arbitrary and the estimate and review already carry the risk.

## 11. The decision hierarchy, player points, and the measured record (2026-10-26)

**Layers, kept apart.** Every card now carries `decision_layers` (`operational/eggy_intelligence.py decision_layers`): A hockey probability, B market probability (no-vig where the market is two-sided, price-implied with margin otherwise), C model edge, D model trust (calibration level, cases, model-reliability flags), E data quality, F hockey / role / model-below-market contradictions, G price and expected value. Every flag the review can raise belongs to one layer (`FLAG_LAYER`; a test fails on an unregistered name, and an unregistered name counts). `MODEL_ABOVE_MARKET` (the model liking a side MORE than the market) and `PRICE_RISK` are informational: they stay on the card and in the `G` layer but are not evidence the leg will fail, so they do not count toward whether a leg may enter a parlay. `MODEL_BELOW_MARKET` (the model saying the side is overpriced), hockey context, role / opportunity, data quality and the model's own reliability for this leg do count. On the 65 distinct legs recorded Oct 22-25, 40 carried a card-level HIGH contradiction that was only `PRICE_ABOVE_ESTIMATE`; the counted contradiction of every recorded leg is LOW or MODERATE.

**Player points** are a first-class market with threshold-specific evidence. `deploy/validate_points_thresholds.py` walks the skater model forward through 2025-26 and 2026-27 (out of time for the calibration fitted on 2024-25) and scores each threshold, on the same post-split games, against a rule declared before the numbers: 5,000+ rows, log loss no worse than the best baseline, case-weighted mean band error <= 0.02, no 500+ band off by more than 0.04. Result (`docs/validation/points_thresholds.json`): 1+ passes on the live calibration (worst band 0.035); 2+ fails on the live calibration (it under-predicts the upper bands by up to 5 points, the conservative direction) and passes on a recalibration fitted only on games before 2026-02-01 and scored after it (worst band 0.013), which the review applies to points>=2; 3+ passes raw. `ei.points_policy()` reads that file: a threshold that is not enabled is not a parlay leg, and a missing file enables nothing. Points prices are bought only if `NHL_ENGINE_PROP_MARKETS` includes `player_points` (an owner credit decision; the default buys shots only), and at the 55% leg floor in practice only 1+ can qualify. Points legs share the existing dependence logic (same player shots and points strong, goal inside points total, teammates' scoring moderate, team result and player offence moderate, opposing teams unknown).

**Postmortem cause.** A loss's CAUSE is separate from its decision quality. A specific cause (ice time, power play, calibration, environment) comes first; otherwise a loss whose testable facts held and which the engine's own estimate called at least 10% likely is `OUTCOME_WITHIN_ESTIMATE`; a loss with no testable fact is `INSUFFICIENT_FACTS`; a fact that held only in part, or a loss rarer than the estimate allowed, stays `UNKNOWN` and is investigated. Stored assessments carry `ASSESS_VERSION` and are rebuilt when it changes.

**The measured record** (`operational/intelligence_metrics.py`, in the postmortem document, shown on Paper Performance): hit rate against expected with n and z for legs (by band, market and threshold, flagged against unflagged, reused against not), moneyline sides against what the market said by model-market gap, GOOD against QUESTIONABLE tickets and ticket probability bands, weakest leg, flagship, the postmortem's cause counts and UNKNOWN rate, decision quality against outcome, the calibration evidence clock, and the points thresholds. Priced metrics (ROI, expected value, closing-line value) are reported as NOT AVAILABLE: no combined price exists. A band that has over-predicted by 2+ standard errors over 30+ settled legs becomes a standing MODERATE caution (`LIVE_CALIBRATION_OVERCONFIDENT`, model-trust layer) in the next review; nothing rewrites a coefficient.

**Recency / role flags are validated** (`deploy/validate_overlay_signals.py`, `docs/validation/overlay_signals.json`): after a 3-game ice-time drop of 3.2+ minutes, shots-2+ falls 4.8 points and points-1+ 3.6 points short of the player's own earlier record (z -8.0, -6.2); after 0 or 1 of the last 5 at 2+ shots for a player at 55%+, the next game falls 12.7 points short (z -16.5); after a 0.75-minute power-play loss, points-1+ falls 1.6 and shots-2+ 2.8 points short.

## 12. What the history says is worth acting on (2026-10-26)

**Signals are held to the model's own probability.** `deploy/validate_player_signals.py` walks the skater model forward through 2025-26 and 2026-27 (out of time for its calibration) and asks, for each flag the review raises, whether games where it was present fell short of the MODEL's probability. Result (`docs/validation/player_signals.json`): the model already uses recent ice time, power-play time and shot form: `RECENT_FORM_BELOW_MODEL`, `PP_ROLE_LOST` (shots), `MODEL_ABOVE_RECORD` and the 3-game `TOI_DROP` (shots) add nothing beyond it and are shown at LOW, not counted. Four signals DO carry information beyond the model and keep their weight with a measured, downward, conservative adjustment (the single most negative applicable, never a sum): `ROLE_ASSUMPTION` (the model expects 2+ minutes more than his last six games; shots 2+ -1.6 pts, shots 3+ -0.8, points 1+ -3.0, points 2+ -0.5), `TOI_DROP` for points 1+ (-2.3), `POSTMORTEM_ROLE_CAUTION` (a previous game's ice time 4+ minutes under the model; shots 2+ -1.8, points 1+ -1.5) and `POWER_PLAY_ASSUMPTION` for points 2+ (-1.3). Ablations: the opponent environment and ice-time recency help the model; a SLOWER rate half-life (60-90 games against 30) predicts slightly better (log loss -0.0005 to -0.0012), a candidate for a controlled retrain, not applied.

**The shots estimate.** The record held to the model + 0.02 is a worse predictor than the model's own calibrated probability out of time (paired log loss, shots 2+: -0.0079, shots 3+: -0.0202, intervals entirely on the model's side), so shots legs are estimated by the model's probability (held to 70% for 3+), under the basis `VALIDATED_MODEL_PROBABILITY`. The player's record stays on the card, not a ceiling.

**Team strength** is one authoritative probability: the team model every review uses (`eggy-matchup-v1`; gd + xgd + high-danger + back-to-back, half-life 30 games), shown on the pages too. `deploy/validate_team_signals.py` (`docs/validation/team_signals.json`) tested what else might improve it over 2017-2025: recent 5-game form, shot-attempt share, rest days, finishing, power play and penalty kill add nothing demonstrable; removing high-danger chances or back-to-back hurts; half-lives of 10 or 60+ games hurt, 15 is indistinguishable, 30 stays.

**Dependence is measured** (`deploy/validate_dependence.py`, `docs/validation/dependence_lift.json`): same-game pairs hit together against what independence predicts, RELATIVE to a cross-game control (the model's calibration bias would otherwise read as dependence). Shots-shots (teammates or opposing), shots-points and a team result with its own players' shots are independent; points-points teammates +11%, a team result with its own players' points +17%, with the OPPOSING side's points -25%. A same-game ticket now carries an evidence-adjusted combined probability (the product times the measured pair lifts, held inside the assumption-free bounds), level `MEASURED_DEPENDENCE`.

**The day is a portfolio** (`portfolio_summary`): distinct legs, the most reused leg, team and game exposure, tickets with a role-flagged leg, and by simulation the expected tickets hit, the chance none hit and how much shared legs widen the spread (1.26 to 1.44 on Oct 22-25). Rules (reuse cap of 2, wave caps, 3-leg preference, GOOD against QUESTIONABLE) are unchanged: the samples are too small to move them, and the record that would decide them is now measured daily.

**Moneyline trust is earned prospectively** (`operational/prospective_moneyline.py`): every finished priced game is added once to a ledger with the hockey model's pregame probability and the market's. On the first 116 games: market log loss 0.6506, hockey 0.6538 (paired z -0.27), strength 0.6504, Elo 0.6748; no gap band has 100 sides, so no disagreement is trusted yet and the estimate stays the market's. Each band's state (`DISAGREEMENT_HAS_SIGNAL`, `DISAGREEMENT_IS_OVERCONFIDENCE`, `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`) is on the card.

**The engine continues after November 2.** The 14-day window stays identifiable (`window: EXPERIMENT` / `PRODUCTION`, one ledger, one cohort, day numbers continue); recording, settlement, the postmortem and the measured record carry on. The calibration evidence is refreshed by `operational/evidence_refresh.py`: a candidate and a proposal are prepared by the daily postmortem job at 14 days (limit 45), and putting one in force is `python3 -m deploy.promote_evidence` followed by the usual activation with the new digest.

**Player points** are validated per threshold (section 11), judged on the validated product model (the older points model's number is kept on the card, unused), and the one credit-consuming step is `NHL_ENGINE_POINTS_FIRST_CLASS=on` (default OFF): points replace anytime goals in each game's capture at the same three credits a game. Goals cannot reach the 55% leg floor, so no goal leg is a parlay ingredient.

## 13. Audit corrections (2026-10-26)

The read-only audit of the promoted engine found defects where intelligence existed but did not decide, or decided twice. What changed, and where it is proved:

* **The portfolio decides.** `learning_parlays.build` no longer takes tickets in rank order. The best remaining ticket sets the bar; every ticket of the same quality and length whose weakest leg is NOT DISTINGUISHABLE from it is "similarly strong"; among those the one adding the least concentration is taken (`concentration_key`: shared legs, other legs of a player already staked, covariance of the tickets' results at the measured lifts, repeated games, repeated teams, shared role uncertainty, shared model uncertainty). A clearly stronger ticket is never passed over; the candidate set, the quality gate, the reuse cap of 2 and the two-leg difference are unchanged. "Not distinguishable" (`similar_strength`) is an uncertainty test, not a borrowed constant: two weakest legs are similar when their estimates differ by less than `Z_SIGNIFICANT` (2) standard errors of the difference, each leg's error being the standard error of the calibration cell its own estimate stands on (median 0.011, so the band is about 3 points); with no such evidence the comparison is strict, by estimate.
* **One ticket, one slot.** The slot test in `build` counted each chosen ticket twice (a call topped out at about five), and the search pool held 14 legs (28 leg slots under the reuse cap of 2: at most nine three-leg tickets). `build` now stops at `slots` tickets in total (recorded plus chosen), and the pool is 18 legs (`POOL_LEGS`: 15 is the exact minimum for ten tickets, 18 is what a one-pass search needs to reach ten on pools of equally strong legs). The day's volume is an output: it is the number of tickets that pass the gates and the reuse and difference rules, at most ten, further limited at recording time by the wave caps.
* **One portfolio assessment.** `leg_view` builds a leg the same way from a candidate and from a stored row; `portfolio_summary` is the one function the search, the status and the postmortem call. Role and model risk come from the card (`leg_risks`), not from a constant.
* **The simulation respects measured dependence.** Same-game legs share a latent game state whose loadings reproduce each measured pairwise lift exactly for two legs and approximately (least squares) for three; cross-game legs are independent by assumption. `chance_no_ticket_hits` is reported with the range it takes when the lifts sit at the ends of their 95% intervals.
* **One fact is counted once.** `eggy_intelligence.CAUSE_FAMILY` groups flags by the underlying hockey fact (a role/ice-time change, a power-play loss, production below the model, the model's overconfidence, the team picture, rest). The contradiction counts each family once at its strongest level; every detector's trace stays on the card (`contradiction.families`). Independent families still compound.
* **Parlay legs are judged as parlay legs, independently of the gate.** `eggy_decisions.parlay_leg_quality` (assessment version 4) reconstructs the decision from pregame information only (the recorded card, the recorded leg, the day's reuse); it has no outcome parameter. BAD: what the builder's own gate refuses, a moneyline whose estimate is a quote the system classes stale, an estimate two standard errors above what its own 300+-case analogue history did, validated flag evidence (built before the decision) that was not applied and would put the estimate under the floor, a player with fewer prior games than the model's validation required, two or more available data items missing. QUESTIONABLE: a counted MODERATE contradiction, an estimate under 62%, 75% or more on calibration below HIGH, one available item missing, a thin record, the same uncertainty staked on two tickets. GOOD: none of these. A leg is not "lucky" for lacking a value thesis; a bad decision can win and a good one can lose.
* **Lessons have states.** `lesson_state`: ACTIONABLE (a named reader consumes it; a test enforces it), ACCUMULATING (the threshold is written down; a rule or policy change is only ever a proposal to the owner), REPORT_ONLY. `_load_overlay_inputs` consumes only the actionable ones.
* **Moneyline trust counts games.** Each game is one observation, oriented to the side the model likes more than the market; the bands partition the games (`prospective_moneyline.BANDS`). The 100-game bars are unchanged.
* **Decision artifacts are policy.** `decision_artifacts` pins `player_signals`, `points_thresholds` and `dependence_lift` by checksum in the cohort policy digest; `artifact_manifest.json` says what each decides and how to regenerate it.
* **Evidence refresh has no calendar cliff.** `evidence_refresh`: a candidate is built, validated and compared every 14 days; a routine one rolls forward by itself into a hash-chained, append-only `evidence_history.jsonl` (the old file is archived); a MATERIAL change (identity, coefficient or cell drift beyond tolerance, less data, failed validation) waits for `deploy.promote_evidence --approve "..."`. The policy digest pins the evidence IDENTITY, not its bytes. The engine refuses evidence its history cannot account for, and stops only when the evidence has gone 45 days unverified.
* **Player Points 1+ is the actionable threshold.** `points_policy()["actionable"]` = validated thresholds with at least 200 validation cases at or above the 55% leg floor: [1]. 2+ and 3+ stay validated and measured but are research only and are never offered as live legs.
* **Goalie information is rejected by evidence** (`deploy/validate_goalie_signal.py`, `docs/validation/goalie_signal.json`): persistent but adds nothing demonstrable to team win probability or points out of time.
* **The parlay product owns its daily maximum** (`learning_cohort.PARLAY_DAILY_MAX`), not the retired straight-bet target. The health check shows the primary slip as RETIRED history and the cohort's policy digest as the live recording system.
* **Replay:** `deploy/replay_corrections.py` and `docs/validation/replay_corrections.json`.

## 14. Price freshness is admission intelligence (2026-10-26)

A sportsbook quote that is hours old is not the current market, and a moneyline's estimate IS its quote. `operational/price_freshness.py` classifies every reviewed leg's price before a parlay can use it, per market, from `docs/validation/price_freshness.json` (built by `deploy/analyze_price_freshness.py`, pinned by the cohort policy digest):

* **FRESH**: age at most `fresh_max` = `CAPTURE_TARGET_MIN + WAVE_MINUTES` = 150 minutes, the oldest a price can be at any game's start when the capture schedule runs as designed (`operational/capture_timeline.py`). No mark on the card; behaviour exactly as before.
* **AGING**: past that but within `aging_max`, and no newer scheduled capture for the game is overdue. Admitted with a visible `PRICE_AGING` note (informational; counts toward no contradiction). `aging_max` is evidence: the last age bucket whose 90th-percentile price move stays within one standard error of the estimate the price feeds. Moneyline: 240 minutes (p90 move 0.0086 at 2-4 h, 0.0109 at 4-8 h, against a standard error of 0.0098 on 4,924 snapshots). Shots, points and saves: no bucket qualifies, so no AGING band (their limit is the schedule's 150).
* **STALE**: older than that, or a newer scheduled capture was due (target + 30 minutes) and does not exist. The leg is still REVIEWED (its probability, contradiction and calibration are untouched) but is not admitted: reason `STALE MARKET PRICE / MARKET DATA QUALITY`, kept apart from every hockey and value reason, and reported with the leg's age, capture time, start and hockey estimate in the day's shortfall.
* **MISSING**: no real observation time; it cannot be called current.

HOCKEY PROBABILITY is not MARKET DATA QUALITY is not PRICE / VALUE. `docs/validation/replay_price_freshness.json` is the replay.

## 15. Eggy Pick of the Day: one NHL side, moneyline or puck line, or PASS (2026-10-26)

Eggy is now a separate product from the learning-parlay engine, which is unchanged (cohort digest `aea7cf0fbcbc632f`): up to 10 unpriced learning parlays a day keep recording, settling, postmortem-ing and learning. Eggy makes at most ONE straight side per day, a MONEYLINE or a PUCK LINE (+/-1.5), or PASS. No props, no points, no shots, no parlay.

* **Decision** (`operational/eggy_pick.py`): once a day, after the pregame league pull (moneyline + puck-line prices) is in and before the first game; with no current pull before the first game the answer is PASS (no current prices). Every game's four sides (two teams x two markets) are evaluated with the authoritative hockey probability, the sportsbook's no-vig probability, the model-versus-market gap, the model-trust state, calibration, data completeness and price freshness.
* **The standard (V2): the market is intelligence, not an opponent to beat first.** Eggy does not need to prove the proprietary model beats the sportsbook in a gap band before it may think. It weighs, in order: A. the market baseline (the fresh no-vig price as a prior); B. hockey support (team strength, xG and goal differential, home, rest / back-to-back); C. price quality against the evidence; D. market behaviour (earliest vs current price, direction, magnitude, when; never called "sharp money", no splits invented); E. the model-market gap, an important signal whose prospective trust sets HOW MUCH it counts, not whether the side may be considered: PROVEN -> a STRONG reason, ACCUMULATING / INSUFFICIENT -> UNPROVEN supporting context at the record's own bounded blend weight (it can never carry a pick alone), FAILS / OVERCONFIDENT -> weight 0 and a reason AGAINST trusting the model; F. the case for and against. The evidence-weighted probability is `market + w x (model - market)` (w = 1 proven, 0 failing, else the record's maximum-likelihood blend estimate bounded to [0, 0.5]).
* **Three evidence structures** (the strongest applicable is the classification; none = not a pick): TYPE B *Supported market gap* (a proven gap that is material, with a positive edge); TYPE C *Market / situational opportunity* (sound hockey picture, H within 2 pts of the market, at least two hockey drivers, at least two measured facts among price moved toward the side / rest advantage / evidence-weighted probability above break-even, the market not moving against it, and a price within 1 pt of what the evidence supports; narrative alone is not evidence); TYPE A *Consensus quality* (market >= 50%, hockey >= 52%, within 5 pts of each other, price in the preferred region, at least two hockey drivers; claims NO positive expected value: the bookmaker's margin is the cost and the card says so). A structure the prospective record has shown to FAIL (|z| >= 2 below the market over 100 games) stops qualifying.
* **Price policy.** The near-even region (-130 to +130) is a PREFERENCE that ranks sides, not a rule that qualifies them; the +/-200 extreme guard and every numeric threshold that has not been measured are labelled HEURISTIC in the policy. The earlier shoulder rule (-160 / twice the edge) is retired: it was an unmeasured heuristic. A stale price is a hard blocker (market data quality), as are weak data or calibration.
* **Case for / against** (`cases`, `balance`): items {kind, category, strength, text}; categories MARKET / HOCKEY / PRICE / MARKET_BEHAVIOUR / MODEL / SITUATION / DATA; strengths STRONG / MODERATE / WEAK / UNPROVEN (shown, never counted). Heuristic rule: no STRONG reason against; MODERATE-or-better reasons for from at least two distinct categories; (strong + moderate for) at least (2 x strong + moderate against) + 2. Confidence is a category (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW), never a percentage.
* **Moneyline or puck line**: ranked by evidence structure, then the preferred price region, then the CONSERVATIVE edge (edge minus the side's own uncertainty), then the case balance; ties to the moneyline. The puck-line probability is the model's win probability mapped through a goal-margin calibration validated out of time (`deploy/build_eggy_pick_calibration.py`, `docs/validation/eggy_pick_calibration.json`, pinned in the Eggy digest). History may lower a probability, never raise it: a bucket that over-predicted out of time (for example 0.55-0.60, by about 4.7 points) lowers the cover probability by that bias and widens the uncertainty. A puck line is never preferred for a larger nominal gap.
* **Reported every day**: the top three researched sides with team, market, price, market and model probabilities, gap, trust state, hockey case, market / price case, case against, evidence classification and whether it qualifies; then the pick or PASS with the specific reasons. Outcomes are never an input to a decision; the replay reads results only afterwards, as shadow information.
* **Market data**: AVAILABLE: DraftKings moneyline and puck-line prices at each league pull, movement since the day's first capture (not the true opening line), price freshness. NOT AVAILABLE, and never manufactured: ticket and handle percentages, their divergence, reverse line movement and market resistance (they need the splits), cross-book disagreement (one book), true opening and closing lines, confirmed starting goalies, reliable lineup and injury information.
* **Record and learning** (`operational/eggy_pick_ledger.py`): its own append-only ledger (decision locked once a day; one OBSERVATION per game with both markets and every signal; OUTCOMES), its own postmortem (decision quality GOOD / QUESTIONABLE / BAD graded from the stored decision, never the result, paired with WIN / LOSS), and its own signal trust over every evidence structure, price range (preferred vs outside), model-market gap (moneyline and puck line), movement toward / away, rest advantage, moneyline vs puck line and confidence category, each measured against the market's price with n and z, driving a decision only after 100 independent games and |z| >= 2. Small samples are shown, never acted on.
