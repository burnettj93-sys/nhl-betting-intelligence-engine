# MoneyPuck Offensive / Defensive Shot-Quality Decomposition — Report

**This turn is a controlled research experiment only.** No production
file was modified. `models/elo_model.py`, `models/combined_model.py`,
`config.py`, pricing/threshold/decision code, and `nhl.db` are all
untouched (confirmed unchanged by mtime). All new work lives under
`research/` plus one new test file. Full suite: **439 / 439 passing**
(421 pre-existing + 18 new).

Every number in this report is generated by
`research/run_shot_quality_comparison.py` and persisted verbatim in
`research/shot_quality_comparison_results.json` — if this document and
that file ever disagree, the JSON file is authoritative.

**Headline finding, stated up front because it's the most important
result of this experiment**: the defensive candidate looked genuinely
promising in tuning (Brier 0.23456 vs. baseline's ~0.238, with a
meaningful fitted coefficient) — and then **completely failed to
replicate on true evaluation data**, flipping sign between the two
evaluation seasons and landing at an aggregate effect statistically
indistinguishable from zero. This is exactly the failure mode the
tuning/true-evaluation split exists to catch, and it caught it. Details
in Sections N-T.

## B. xGF/60 formula

```
xGF/60 = ( Σ xg_for over the most recent `window` season-scoped,
           strictly-prior 5v5 games ) * 3600 / Σ ice_time_seconds
```

## C. xGA/60 formula

```
xGA/60 = ( Σ xg_against over the most recent `window` season-scoped,
           strictly-prior 5v5 games ) * 3600 / Σ ice_time_seconds
```
Lower xGA/60 = a **better** defense.

**Pooled, not averaged (Part 13)**: the numerator and denominator are
each summed across the window *before* dividing once — verified this
matters with a dedicated test
(`test_xgf60_pooled_not_averaged`): a synthetic case with one
low-TOI/high-xG game and one high-TOI/low-xG game produces a materially
different result under pooling vs. naive per-game averaging, and the
pooled formula is the one implemented.

## D. Rolling windows tested

`{10, 25}` for offense and defense independently (Part 5) — both
selected **window 25** on tuning-season Brier (`offense_defense_same_window:
true`), so no cross-speed-of-maturity divergence was found between
offense and defense in this data.

## E. Maturity policy

Same full-window-required policy as the other two MoneyPuck feature
modules, plus a nominal `MIN_TOTAL_TOI_SECONDS = 600` (10 minutes) floor
inherited from the special-teams precedent. **This floor is essentially
never binding at 5v5** — 5v5 TOI is abundant (~49 min/game vs.
~4.6 min/game for a single special-teams unit), so the real maturity
gate here is the game-count requirement, not TOI scarcity, unlike
special teams where the TOI floor was a genuine safety backstop.
Reported explicitly (Part 6) rather than silently inherited without
re-justifying it for this different data regime.

## F. Season-boundary policy

Identical no-cross-season-carryover policy as the other two MoneyPuck
feature modules, for the same simplicity/auditability reasons.

## G. Matchup terms

```
term_home = home_offense_xGF60 − away_defense_xGA60
term_away = away_offense_xGF60 − home_defense_xGA60
```
Distinguishes "strong offense vs. weak defense" from "weak offense vs.
strong defense" (Part 3) — a genuine opponent-specific quantity, not two
independent team ratings.

**On Part 4's redundancy check**: B, C, D, and E are **not**
mathematically redundant with each other by construction — B/C are
single-feature team-level ratings, D is a joint 2-parameter fit over the
*same* two team-level differentials (testing whether combining changes
the fitted weights vs. either alone), and E replaces the team-level
differentials with opponent-specific matchup terms entirely. They *are*,
however, substantially correlated with each other and with the
already-tested 5v5 xG share feature — see Section Y, which is the more
important redundancy finding this experiment actually surfaced.

## H. Integration formula

Identical mechanism reused from both prior xG-family experiments: `p_candidate
= sigmoid(logit(p_baseline) + Σ beta_j · z_j)`. Baseline is the exact
same Elo-only trajectory reused unchanged from all three prior
experiments (Part 10/11 — none of MOV Elo, OT/SO Elo, 5v5 xG share, the
xG form-delta, or any PP/PK candidate is folded into this baseline).

## T. Season-by-season results (common evaluation set)

| Candidate | 2024-25 Δ Brier | 2025-26 Δ Brier |
|---|---|---|
| B | -0.000006 | -0.000080 |
| **C** | **+0.000421** | **-0.000426** |
| D | +0.000557 | +0.000186 |
| E | +0.002274 | +0.001414 |

**C's aggregate near-zero result is not "consistently negligible" — it
is two meaningfully-sized effects of opposite sign that happen to
roughly cancel** (worse by +0.000421 in 2024-25, better by -0.000426 in
2025-26). That is the clearest possible signature of a coefficient
fit to tuning-season noise rather than a real, stable effect: it doesn't
even agree with itself across the two held-out seasons, let alone match
its own tuning-season promise. B is directionally consistent across both
seasons but the magnitude is negligible in both. D and E are
consistently worse in both seasons — real, repeatable harm, not noise.

## W / X. Offense and defense stability (Part 22, compared explicitly against special teams)

| Metric | Offense (xGF/60) | Defense (xGA/60) | Special teams (PP xGF/60, for reference) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rolling volatility (window-10 mean coefficient of variation) | **0.098** | **0.092** | 0.268 |
| Season-to-season persistence r (window 25, 2023-24 → 2024-25) | **0.264** | **0.291** | 0.105 |
| Rank persistence r | 0.291 | 0.317 | — (not computed previously) |

**Both offense and defense are meaningfully more stable than the
special-teams signal** — roughly a third of the rolling-window
volatility, and roughly 2.5-2.8x the season-to-season persistence.
This is exactly what should be expected given the far larger 5v5 TOI
sample per team-game. But **"more stable than special teams" is not the
same as "stable enough to be reliably predictive"**: r≈0.26-0.32 still
means the vast majority of season-to-season variance in these rates is
NOT explained by a persistent team quality — roughly 91-93% of the
variance is something other than a carried-forward skill signal at this
level of measurement. Part 32 condition 8 (stability materially better
than special teams) is **satisfied** in relative terms for both
components — but that alone doesn't rescue the effect-size and
season-consistency failures found in Sections N-T, particularly for C,
whose own within-experiment season-to-season behavior (Section T) is
far more erratic than this aggregate stability number would suggest.

## Y. Correlation findings (Part 23, tuning season)

| Pair | r |
|---|---|
| Offense diff vs. 5v5 xG share (already-tested feature) | **0.848** |
| Defense diff vs. 5v5 xG share | **0.794** |
| Offense diff vs. Elo log-odds | 0.506 |
| Defense diff vs. Elo log-odds | 0.554 |
| Offense diff vs. baseline p(home) | 0.505 |
| Defense diff vs. baseline p(home) | 0.553 |
| Offense diff vs. defense diff (each other) | 0.356 |

**This is the real redundancy finding of this experiment.** Both
offense and defense components are **very strongly correlated with the
5v5 xG share feature already tested and rejected** — unsurprising on
reflection, since 5v5 xG share (`xGF / (xGF+xGA)`) is mathematically a
direct function of exactly these two quantities. Decomposing xG share
into its two components does **not** introduce a fundamentally new
signal (Part 23's explicit question) — it is substantially a
re-parameterization of the same underlying information the project
already tested. Offense and defense are only moderately correlated with
*each other* (r=0.356), so the decomposition is not internally
redundant — but externally, relative to what this project has already
tried, it's mostly covering the same ground.

## AD. Recommendation

```
KEEP CURRENT MODEL.
```

Checked against all 10 of Part 32's conditions for the nominal "best"
candidate (B):
1. Brier improves — technically yes, by -0.018%.
2. Log loss improves/neutral — technically yes, by -0.012%.
3. Persists across both seasons — yes, directionally, but both
   magnitudes are negligible.
4. Calibration acceptable — yes (no material change either way).
5. Extremes controlled — yes.
6. **Meaningful (not microscopic) effect — fails outright.** Smaller
   than the already-rejected 5v5 xG share candidate.
7. Credible bootstrap support — the interval is tight and consistent,
   but around a point estimate too small to matter.
8. Stability materially better than special teams — yes for the
   underlying components in isolation, but this doesn't rescue #6.
9. Contributes information beyond Elo — **fails**: r=0.51-0.55 with
   Elo's own log-odds, and r=0.79-0.85 with the already-tested 5v5 xG
   share feature (Section Y) — this is mostly redundant with signal
   already examined, not new information.
10. Clean temporal integrity — pass.

B fails on effect size and novelty (conditions 6 and 9) even though it
technically "wins" on point estimates. C is the more instructive
failure: a real-looking tuning-season result (β=0.124, meaningful Brier
improvement) that **completely failed to replicate**, flipping sign
between the two evaluation seasons — a clean demonstration of exactly
what the tuning/evaluation discipline is for. D and E are not close
calls; combining and matchup-interaction formulations both made things
measurably worse, with E showing the strongest, most consistent negative
result of any candidate tested across all four experiments in this
project.

## AE. Recommended next single development slice

Given this experiment's own finding that offense/defense decomposition
is substantially redundant with the already-tested 5v5 xG share (Section
Y), and that neither the combined nor matchup-aware formulations added
value, **the shot-quality/xG feature family (as built from these
MoneyPuck team-game-by-game inputs) has now been examined from three
independent angles — combined share, PP/PK, and offense/defense split —
without finding an adoptable signal.** Continuing to re-slice the same
underlying xG data is unlikely to be the highest-value next step per
Part 31's standing guidance from the special-teams experiment (don't
keep tuning increasingly complicated formulas on data that doesn't
support them).

**Recommended next slice: build the research/backtest-process dashboard**
(Part 33/AF below) rather than a fifth MoneyPuck feature variant. The
project now has four rigorously-documented real-data experiments, a
validated architecture, and a clear, honest research narrative — that is
itself a valuable thing to make visible, and doing so doesn't require
waiting for a fifth attempt at the same underlying data source to
succeed.
